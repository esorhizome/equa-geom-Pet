## Lines that were wrong

### Thunder

- **Before:** Thunder is the air slamming shut | after lightning pushed it apart.
- **After:** Thunder is air bursting outward. | Lightning heats it | in a split second.
- **Why:** The "slamming shut" idea (a vacuum collapsing) was the accepted theory in the mid-1800s. Today, thunder is understood as a shock wave from air expanding very fast.
- **Source:** [Thunder, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunder)

---

### Ways to tie a tie

- **Before:** There are 85 ways to tie a tie. | Someone counted.
- **After:** In 1999, mathematicians counted | 85 ways to tie a tie. | A 2014 recount found 177,147.
- **Why:** Fink and Mao counted 85 in 1999, using strict rules about how a knot must finish. A later team relaxed those rules and counted 177,147.
- **Sources:**
  - Hirsch, Markström, Patterson, Sandberg and Vejdemo-Johansson (2015), [*More ties than we thought*](https://peerj.com/articles/cs-2), PeerJ Computer Science
  - [There Are 177,147 Ways To Tie A Tie](https://popsci.com/article/science/there-are-177147-ways-tie-tie), Popular Science

## Lines that said more than is true

### Fireflies

- **Before:** Fireflies make light with almost no heat. | The most efficient lamp | we know of.
- **After:** Fireflies glow with almost no heat. | Cold light, | made by a chemical reaction.
- **Why:** The old figure of near 100% efficiency was remeasured in 2008 at about 41%. Still remarkable, but not "the most efficient lamp".
- **Source:** Ando et al. (2008), [*Firefly bioluminescence quantum yield and colour change by pH-sensitive green emission*](https://www.nature.com/articles/nphoton.2007.251), Nature Photonics

---

### Fibonacci in flowers

- **Before:** Count the petals on a daisy. | Often 13, 21 or 34. | All Fibonacci numbers.
- **After:** Count the seed spirals on a sunflower. | Usually two neighbouring Fibonacci numbers, | like 34 and 55.
- **Why:** Fibonacci petal counts are a popular claim that holds only loosely. Sunflower seed spirals have been measured: in the largest study, about three quarters of the spiral counts were Fibonacci numbers.
- **Source:** Swinton and Ochu (2016), [*Novel Fibonacci and non-Fibonacci structure in the sunflower*](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.160091), Royal Society Open Science

---

### Zero and the Greeks

- **Before:** The Greeks had no symbol for zero. | Try long division without it.
- **After:** Roman numerals have no zero. | Try long division in them.
- **Why:** Greek astronomers did use a zero symbol. Ptolemy used one around the year 130. Roman numerals truly have none.
- **Source:** [A history of Zero](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/Zero/), MacTutor History of Mathematics, University of St Andrews

---

### Made of stardust

- **Before:** You are made of atoms | that were made inside stars | that died long ago.
- **After:** The carbon and oxygen in you | were made inside stars | long ago.
- **Why:** Most of the atoms in a body are hydrogen, and hydrogen came from the Big Bang, not from stars. Carbon and oxygen were made inside stars.
- **Source:** [Where your elements came from](https://chandra.si.edu/chemistry/images/Poster5.pdf), NASA Chandra X-ray Center

---

### Hot water freezing faster

- **Before:** Hot water can sometimes freeze faster than cold. | Nobody fully agrees why.
- **After:** Hot water sometimes seems to freeze | faster than cold. | Scientists still argue whether, and why.
- **Why:** Scientists disagree about whether the effect (the Mpemba effect) really exists, not only about why.
- **Sources:**
  - Burridge and Linden (2016), [*Questioning the Mpemba effect: hot water does not cool more quickly than cold*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5121640), Scientific Reports
  - [Frosty reception for claim that effect where hot water freezes faster than cold has no basis](https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/mpemba-effect-in-hot-water-after-doubt-cast-on-its-existence/2500087.article), Chemistry World

---

### Seeing a single photon

- **Before:** Your eye can catch a single photon. | The dimmest thing there is.
- **After:** Your eye can sometimes sense | a single photon. | The smallest bit of light there is.
- **Why:** In a 2016 experiment, people sensed single photons better than chance, but not every time.
- **Source:** Tinsley et al. (2016), [*Direct detection of a single photon by humans*](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms12172), Nature Communications

---

### Satellites and time

- **Before:** Clocks tick slower when they move fast. | Satellites have to correct for it | or your map would drift.
- **After:** Time runs at a different speed | for satellites. | GPS corrects for it, or maps would drift.
- **Why:** GPS satellite clocks are slowed a little by their speed, but sped up more by weaker gravity. Overall they run fast, by about 38 microseconds a day. Uncorrected, positions would drift by around 10 km a day.
- **Source:** [Relativistic Effects on Satellite Clock as Seen from Earth](https://demonstrations.wolfram.com/RelativisticEffectsOnSatelliteClockAsSeenFromEarth/), Wolfram Demonstrations Project

### Thirty-nine digits of pi

- **Before:** Pi is known to trillions of digits. | Thirty-nine would measure the universe.
- **After:** Pi is known to trillions of digits. | Thirty-nine would measure the universe | to the width of an atom.
- **Why:** The claim needs its precision stated: 39 digits give the circumference of the visible universe to within about the width of a hydrogen atom.
- **Sources:**
  - [How Many Decimals of Pi Do We Really Need?](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/edu/news/2016/3/16/how-many-decimals-of-pi-do-we-really-need/), NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory
  - [How Many Digits Of Pi Do We Really Need?](https://www.sciencefriday.com/segments/how-many-digits-of-pi-do-we-really-need/), Science Friday

---

### Euler

- **Before:** Euler wrote more maths than anyone. | Much of it after he went blind.
- **After:** Euler is one of the most prolific | mathematicians ever. | Nearly half came after he lost his sight.
- **Why:** Euler is widely called the most prolific mathematician, but "more than anyone" depends on how you count. Nearly half his work came after he went almost totally blind in 1766.
- **Source:** [Leonhard Euler](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/SH/euler_sh.pdf), MacTutor History of Mathematics, University of St Andrews

---

### The plus sign

- **Before:** The plus sign grew out of "et", | Latin for "and", | written in a hurry.
- **After:** The plus sign most likely grew from "et", | Latin for "and", | written in a hurry.
- **Why:** This is the accepted origin, but the earliest examples may be a copyist's, so "most likely" is the honest wording.
- **Source:** [Earliest Uses of Symbols of Operation](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Miller/mathsym/operation/), MacTutor History of Mathematics, University of St Andrews

## Lines made more precise

These did not need a source. The old wording went further than the fact, or was vague.

---

### Maths

- **Times 9:** "any whole number" became "any number from 1 up". *Zero times 9 is 0, so it never lands on 9.*
- **Halve or triple:** "Everyone lands on 1" became "Every number tried lands on 1". *This is the Collatz conjecture. It has never been proved.*
- **Twelve:** dropped "that is why eggs come in twelves and clocks have twelve hours". *A good guess, but not a known cause.*
- **Square's diagonal:** "never a neat fraction" became "never a neat fraction of its side", and "shocked the Greeks" became "legend says". *The story of the shock is a legend.*
- **Platonic solids:** "the shape of space decides" became "a corner can only hold so many faces". *That is the real reason there are only five.*
- **Googol:** "fewer atoms than that nearby" became "the visible universe has fewer atoms". *The usual estimate is about 1 followed by 80 zeros.*
- **Rice on a chessboard:** "a country" became "a small country". *Closer to the real amount.*
- **Four:** added "In English". *In other languages it doesn't work.*
- **Shortest path:** the riddle about bent space became a plain line about geodesics.
- **Eleven trick:** "any two-digit number" became one worked example, 11 x 23 = 253. *The trick breaks when the two digits add to 10 or more.*
- **3-4-5 triangle:** dropped the knotted-rope story. *It is popular, but the historical evidence is thin.*
- **Hundred-sided shape:** "exactly like a circle" became "just like a circle".
- **Circle into wedges:** "a rectangle" became "almost a rectangle". *It only becomes a true rectangle as the wedges get infinitely thin.*
- **Even numbers as two primes:** added "above 2". *This is Goldbach's conjecture. 2 itself doesn't fit it.*
- **Alphabet:** "an alphabet" became "the English alphabet".
- **Prime fingerprint:** added "whole number above 1". *1 has no prime factors.*
- **Cardioid:** added "a coin the same size". *Different sizes trace different curves.*
- **Infinity:** "not a number, a direction" became "comes in sizes". *The first was poetic but arguable. The second is Cantor's result.*

---

### Physics

- **Faster than light:** "because of how time works" became "the closer it gets, the more each push costs". *Plainer, and closer to the physics.*
- **Power stations:** "every" became "nearly every". *Solar panels make electricity without magnets.*
- **Ice floats:** "almost nothing else" became "very few things". *A few other substances, like silicon and gallium, also expand when they freeze.*
- **The Sun:** "into light" became "into energy". *Not all of it leaves as light.*
- **Bicycle:** added "mostly". *Several effects help a bike stay up. Steering is the main one.*
- **Shadows:** "blurs the sharpest shadow" became "faint ripples". *Most shadow blur comes from the size of the light source, not diffraction.*
- **Mains electricity:** "hums" became "swings back and forth". *Mains completes 50 or 60 cycles a second. The hum you hear is often twice that.*
- **Superconductors:** "forever, losing nothing" became "for years, losing almost nothing".

## Check the numbers yourself

Every line with arithmetic in it was checked by this script. To run it, save it as `check.py`, then run `python check.py`. Each line should start with `ok`.

```python
import math, itertools

checks = [
    ("111,111,111 squared", 111111111**2 == 12345678987654321),
    ("1089 times 9 is 9801", 1089 * 9 == 9801),
    ("153 is its own digits cubed", 1**3 + 5**3 + 3**3 == 153),
    ("1+2+3+4+5+6+7 + 8x9 is 100", sum(range(1, 8)) + 8 * 9 == 100),
    ("35 times 35 is 1225", 35 * 35 == 1225),
    ("11 times 23 is 253", 11 * 23 == 253),
    ("1001 is 7 x 11 x 13", 7 * 11 * 13 == 1001),
    ("repeated 3 digits divide by 1001", all(int(str(n) * 2) % 1001 == 0 for n in range(100, 1000))),
    ("1 to 100 adds to 5050", sum(range(1, 101)) == 5050),
    ("one seventh repeats 142857", str(10**12 // 7) == "142857142857"),
    ("squares end in 0,1,4,5,6,9", sorted({n * n % 10 for n in range(100)}) == [0, 1, 4, 5, 6, 9]),
    ("multiples of 9 reduce to 9", all((9 * n - 1) % 9 + 1 == 9 for n in range(1, 10000))),
    ("odd numbers add to squares", all(sum(range(1, 2 * k, 2)) == k * k for k in range(1, 50))),
    ("six ways to roll a 7", sum(1 for a, b in itertools.product(range(1, 7), repeat=2) if a + b == 7) == 6),
    ("23 people: shared birthday over half", 1 - math.prod((365 - i) / 365 for i in range(23)) > 0.5),
    ("52 card orders beat seconds since Big Bang", math.factorial(52) > 13.8e9 * 3.156e7),
    ("penny doubled for 30 days: over 5 million", 2**29 / 100 > 5e6),
    ("paper folded 42 times passes the Moon", 0.1e-6 * 2**42 > 384400),
    ("1 metre of slack lifts a ring 16 cm", round(100 / (2 * math.pi)) == 16),
    ("echo from 100 m: about 0.58 s", round(200 / 343, 2) == 0.58),
]

p, steps = 77, []
while p >= 10:
    p = math.prod(int(d) for d in str(p))
    steps.append(p)
checks.append(("77 takes four steps to one digit", steps == [49, 36, 18, 8]))

for name, passed in checks:
    print("ok " if passed else "BAD", name)
```
